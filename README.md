## 1. The field from above

**What to notice:**

- **The center line** (dashed) splits the field: red owns columns A, B, C and blue owns D, E, F. You must **start** fully on your own side (rule G304). *Why:* so robots don't crash into each other the moment AUTO begins.
- **The two HIVES sit in the middle**, side by side. Each one starts with one CELL up holding 3 of that alliance's NECTAR (marked UP). *Why it matters:* every robot drives toward the middle to score, so the center gets crowded.
- **The field is the same if you spin it halfway around.** Red's LOADING ZONE, GARDEN and FLOWERS sit exactly where blue's are after a half turn. *Why it matters:* an AUTO program written for red can be mirrored for blue.
- **Where you may start (G304):** touching the wall, fully on your side, not in the LOADING ZONE, not touching a FLOWER, and touching exactly 4 POLLEN that are fully on your side. The dashed squares are just examples.
- **4 FLOWERS** stand against the walls, each holding 4 POLLEN at the start. Each GARDEN starts with 4 POLLEN. *(manual 10.3.1)*

**Why the two sizes matter:** one intake (the part that grabs balls) has to handle both. A gap that grips a 2.8 in ball might jam on a 3.6 in ball. The manual also warns that the balls are not perfectly round and vary a little in size, so leave some wiggle room.

**Why the limits are boxes, not suggestions:** the robot has to be built so it *physically cannot* grow past the limit (R105). A line of code that says "stop at 29 in" is not enough, because code can have bugs.

The opening of the up CELL is between **53.5 in and 65.6 in** above the floor. A robot may never be taller than **29 in**. That leaves a gap of **24.5 in**, about 2 ft, that the ball has to fly across. So a robot **can't just reach up and drop a ball in. It has to LAUNCH it** (shoot it through the air).

To picture it: 29 in is about the height of a school desk. The bottom of the opening (53.5 in, about 4 and a half feet) is roughly shoulder height on many 8th graders, and the top (65.6 in) is almost 5 and a half feet. Stand next to a wall and mark those heights with tape to feel it.

### AprilTags on the HIVE

The square codes a camera can read sit on the **bottom of each CELL, facing down**. They **move when the HIVE tips**, so use them to **aim**, not to work out where the robot is. For position, use odometry (wheels that measure how far the robot moved).

**What the numbers mean:** every AprilTag carries one ID number, like a jersey number. When the camera sees a tag, the code gets its number, so it knows exactly which CELL it is looking at. Each CELL has a sticker with 4 tags, so the 4 CELLS have 16 tags in all, numbered 30 to 45 *(manual 9.6)*.

- **AUTO (30 s):** the robot runs your team's autonomous program with no gamepads. *Why it matters:* everything the robot does here comes from code, so it has to be tested and timed.
- **The 8 s pause:** no powered motion at all, not even a twitch (G403). Drivers can press INIT for TeleOp, but only if INIT doesn't move anything. *Why:* referees use this pause to count AUTO points, and moving would change them.
- **TELEOP (2:00):** drivers take over. **With 1:00 left**, NECTAR may go into FLOWERS (G410). *Why:* it saves the FLOWER fight for a dramatic ending.
- **At 0:00, stop.** Robots must not move after the match ends (G404). Points are counted once everything stops moving.

### Ranking points (how teams are sorted at a tournament)

Match points decide who wins a match. **Ranking points** (RP) decide who is at the top of the list after all the qualification matches. You can earn RP even in a match you lose.

<div class="gv-rp"><div><span class="gv-big">3</span><b>WIN</b>More match points than the other alliance. A tie gives 1.</div><div><span class="gv-big">1</span><b>SWARM</b>Your alliance gets 16 or more points from LEAVE plus PARK together.</div><div><span class="gv-big">1</span><b>POLLINATOR 1</b>Your alliance gets 4 or more TIPS.</div><div><span class="gv-big">1</span><b>POLLINATOR 2</b>Your alliance gets 7 or more TIPS.</div></div>

Most RP in one match: 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 = **6**. The thresholds (16 points, 4 TIPS, 7 TIPS) are for regular events. Regional Championship and Championship numbers are not announced yet. <span class="gv-src">Table 10-2 and Table 10-3.</span>

**Quick math that shows what matters:**

- Both robots LEAVE and PARK in AUTO: 3 + 3 + 5 + 5 = **16**, which is exactly enough for the SWARM RP. *Why it matters:* a simple, reliable AUTO that drives off the wall and parks is already worth a ranking point.
- 4 TIPS = 80 points and the POLLINATOR 1 RP. Each TIP also earns your human player one more NECTAR.
- Fouls give points to the **other** alliance: a minor foul gives them 5, a major foul gives them 20 (Table 10-4). One major foul hands the other alliance as many points as a whole TIP.

## 6. Rules that bite

These are easy to break by accident, and they shape how the robot is built and coded. Each one is short here; the full wording is in the manual.

<div class="gv-rules"><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G407</span><b>Hold at most 4 balls</b></p><p>Your robot may not control more than 4 balls at once.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> so one robot can't scoop up everything and leave nothing for the others. Build the intake so a 5th ball can't get in.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G417</span><b>Don't push the HIVE</b></p><p>The only way to move a HIVE is to launch balls into its up CELL. No ramming, bumping on purpose, or shooting at its outside.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> the HIVE is a balanced seesaw. Shoving it would let robots fake a TIP or stop the other alliance's TIP.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G409</span><b>Don't catch falling balls</b></p><p>When a HIVE tips, its balls must touch something else (like the floor) before your robot catches or deflects them.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> spilled balls are meant to hit the floor first, so nobody waits under the HIVE with an open basket. Add guards so balls can't land on top of the robot by accident.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G418</span><b>FLOWER: in at the top, out at the bottom</b></p><p>Put balls into a FLOWER only from the top. Take out only POLLEN, and only from the bottom opening.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> the FLOWER works like a stack, so ownership depends on which NECTAR is on top. Pulling balls out the side would cheat the stack.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G410</span><b>NECTAR into FLOWERS only in the last minute</b></p><p>Putting NECTAR into a FLOWER before 1:00 is left is a major foul for each NECTAR.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> the FLOWER battle is the finale. Drivers watch the field timer, and a TeleOp program can buzz the gamepad at 1:00 as a reminder.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G403 and G404</span><b>Freeze during the pause and after the end</b></p><p>No powered movement in the 8 s pause after AUTO or after the match ends. Pressing INIT must not twitch any motor or servo.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> referees count points during these moments. For code: INIT must not move anything, and the AUTO program should finish its job inside the 30 s.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G304</span><b>Still after INIT</b></p><p>At setup your robot touches the wall on its own side and touches exactly 4 POLLEN that are fully on its own side (TU03). It must stay still after INIT.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> every robot starts the same, fair way. For code: INIT must not move anything.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">G408</span><b>Not their NECTAR</b></p><p>Don't control the other alliance's NECTAR.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> each alliance plays with its own color. For code: detect the color and push the wrong one away.</p></div><div class="gv-rule"><p><span class="gv-num">R704.D</span><b>No Dashboard in matches</b></p><p>No FTC Dashboard, Panels or video streaming during matches. Telemetry goes through the Driver Station only.</p><p class="gv-why"><b>Why:</b> event Wi-Fi is shared by every robot. Never put Dashboard in competition code.</p></div></div>
